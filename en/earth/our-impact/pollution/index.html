    Air, water and soil pollution

    The air we breathe, the water we drink and the light that steals our stars: types of pollution, their effects and the successes in fighting it.

    Pollution is the release into the air, water or soil of substances or energy that harm health and ecosystems. It is one of the world's biggest health problems: according to the World Health Organization, air pollution causes about 7 million premature deaths a year.

    Air

    The main air pollutants come from exhaust pipes, coal power stations, industry, heating, and burning wood and crop waste:

    • Fine particles (PM2.5): so small they reach the bloodstream. They are the most harmful.
    • Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂): mainly from diesel engines. It is the typical pollutant of Spanish cities.
    • Ground-level ozone: forms in sunlight from other pollutants. It is a summer problem in many areas.

    99% of the world's population breathes air that exceeds WHO guideline limits. The most polluted cities are in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and China, although China has greatly reduced its pollution since 2013.

    The ozone layer success story

    In 1985 a hole in the ozone layer was discovered over Antarctica, caused by CFCs, gases used in aerosols and fridges. The ozone layer, in the stratosphere, protects us from ultraviolet rays. In 1987 the Montreal Protocol banned these gases worldwide: it is the most successful environmental treaty in history. The layer is recovering and should return to its 1980 state over Antarctica around 2066.

    Water

    About 2 billion people drink contaminated water. Rivers receive untreated sewage, fertilisers and pesticides from farmland, and industrial discharges. Excess nitrates and phosphates cause eutrophication: algae grow out of control, use up the oxygen and the fish die. This happened to the Mar Menor lagoon in Murcia, where tonnes of dead fish washed up in 2019 and 2021. In 2022 it became the first ecosystem in Europe to be granted legal personhood.

    Soil

    Soils are polluted by heavy metals, waste and chemicals. In 1998 a burst tailings dam at the Aznalcóllar mine released millions of cubic metres of toxic sludge that reached the edge of Doñana. It was Spain's worst ecological disaster.

    Pollution you cannot see (and pollution you can hear)

    • Light pollution means about 80% of humanity cannot see the Milky Way from home. It disorients migrating birds, turtles and insects. The Canary Islands have a law protecting the darkness of their skies for the observatories.
    • Noise is the second environmental factor most harmful to health in Europe, after air pollution.
    Did you know?

    Acid rain, Europe's great environmental problem of the 1980s, which left forests and lakes lifeless, fell dramatically thanks to filters on power station chimneys. Environmental problems can be solved when there is the will.

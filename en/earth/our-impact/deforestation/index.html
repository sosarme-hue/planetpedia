    Deforestation and habitat loss

    Why forests are disappearing, the products behind it, wildfires and the countries that have managed to bring their trees back.

    Since the end of the last ice age, humanity has cleared about a third of the planet's forests, half of that in the last century alone. Today about 10 million hectares of forest are still lost every year, mainly in the tropics: an area the size of Portugal.

    What lies behind it

    Most tropical deforestation is driven by farming and ranching:

    • Cattle: the main cause in the Amazon, where forest is burned to create pasture.
    • Soy: most of it is used as animal feed, including in Europe.
    • Palm oil: in Indonesia and Malaysia, plantations have replaced huge areas of rainforest, home to orangutans.
    • Also cocoa, coffee, timber, paper and mining.

    Since 2024 the European Union has required that products such as coffee, cocoa, soy, beef and palm oil sold in Europe must not come from deforested land.

    Habitat loss, the biggest threat

    According to the UN, habitat destruction is the main cause of biodiversity loss, ahead of climate change. When a forest is cut into fragments, its animals are left stranded on "islands" that are too small. Add to this invasive species, carried by humans to places where they have no enemies: the zebra mussel in the Ebro, the American red swamp crayfish, or water hyacinth in the Guadiana.

    Wildfires

    Wildfires are a natural part of many ecosystems, such as Mediterranean ones, but climate change and rural abandonment make them bigger and more dangerous. In 2025 Spain suffered one of its worst fire seasons, with more than 350,000 hectares burned, mostly in Galicia, Castile and León and Extremadura.

    Experts keep repeating that fires are put out in winter: by clearing undergrowth, bringing back grazing and creating mosaic landscapes with crops that slow the flames.

    Good news: forests are coming back

    • Deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon halved between 2022 and 2024.
    • Costa Rica went from 21% forest cover in 1987 to more than 50% today, by paying landowners to conserve it.
    • In Europe, including Spain, forest area has been growing for decades.
    • Africa's Great Green Wall aims to restore 100 million hectares across the Sahel, from Senegal to Djibouti, although it is progressing more slowly than planned.
    Did you know?

    Planting trees is not always the answer: planting the wrong species, or planting in places that were never forest, such as grasslands or peat bogs, can do more harm than good. Protecting the old forest that already exists is far more effective than planting a new one.

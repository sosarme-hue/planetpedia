    Exploring Mars

    Robots that drive, fly and drill: half a century searching for water and life on the red planet, and the dream of sending people.

    No other planet has been visited as often as Mars. But getting there is not easy: almost half of the missions sent have failed, so much so that engineers joke about a "Great Galactic Ghoul" that eats spacecraft.

    Half a century of visits

    MissionYearAchievement
    Mariner 4 (US)1965First close-up photos: a cratered world
    Mars 3 (USSR)1971First landing; transmitted for just 20 seconds
    Viking 1 and 2 (US)1976First photos from the surface and experiments looking for life
    Sojourner (US)1997First wheeled vehicle, the size of a microwave
    Spirit and Opportunity (US)2004Proved there had been liquid water; Opportunity lasted 14 years
    Mars Express (ESA)2003European orbiter; detected ice at the poles
    Curiosity (US)2012Mobile laboratory in Gale crater, still active
    Perseverance and Ingenuity (US)2021Collects samples; first powered flight on another planet
    Zhurong (China)2021China's first Mars rover

    Seven minutes of terror

    Landing on Mars is extremely hard: the atmosphere is so thin that parachutes barely slow you down, yet thick enough to burn the craft. The signal takes between 4 and 24 minutes to reach Earth, so the spacecraft has to do everything on its own. For the 900 kg Curiosity, engineers invented a sky crane: a rocket-powered platform that lowered it to the ground on cables and then flew away.

    Curiosity and Perseverance

    Curiosity showed that Gale crater was once a lake with drinkable water where something microscopic could have lived, and detected organic molecules and puzzling swings in methane. It is still climbing the 5 km high Mount Sharp.

    Perseverance is exploring Jezero crater, an ancient river delta. It has stored dozens of tubes of rock samples to bring back to Earth one day. In 2024 it found spots on a rock called Cheyava Falls that on Earth are usually associated with microbes; it is not proof of life, but it is the most intriguing clue so far.

    Ingenuity, the little helicopter

    Ingenuity weighed 1.8 kg and travelled attached to Perseverance's belly. On 19 April 2021 it made the first powered flight on another planet. It was meant to fly five times; it flew 72, until one of its blades broke in January 2024. It carries a scrap of fabric from the Wright brothers' plane, which made the first powered flight on Earth in 1903.

    People on Mars?

    A crewed trip would take about two and a half years: some seven months out, more than a year waiting for the planets to line up again, and seven months back. The challenges are huge: radiation, bone and muscle loss, isolation, producing oxygen and fuel there. Perseverance's MOXIE instrument has already shown that oxygen can be made from Martian air. Neither NASA nor anyone else yet has a firm date.

    Did you know?

    Curiosity sang itself "Happy Birthday" on its first anniversary on Mars by vibrating its sample arm to play the tune. It was the first song "performed" on another planet.

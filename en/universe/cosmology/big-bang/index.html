    The Big Bang

    13.8 billion years ago, the entire observable universe was packed into an incredibly dense, hot state. This is how it all began.

    The Big Bang theory explains that the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in an extremely hot, dense state, and that it has been expanding and cooling ever since. It was not an explosion at one point in space but the expansion of space itself, everywhere at once.

    A priest and a mocking name

    The idea was put forward in 1927 by the Belgian priest and physicist Georges Lemaître, who spoke of a "primeval atom" from which everything emerged. At first Einstein rejected it. The name Big Bang was coined in 1949 by the British astronomer Fred Hoyle on a radio programme, mockingly, because he championed an eternal universe with no beginning. The nickname stuck.

    The evidence

    The Big Bang theory rests on several independent lines of evidence:

    • Expansion: galaxies are moving away from each other, and the farther they are, the faster. See the expansion of the universe.
    • The cosmic microwave background: light arriving from every direction with exactly the temperature the theory predicts.
    • The mix of elements: the universe is 75% hydrogen and 25% helium, just what forms in the first three minutes.
    • Distant galaxies are different: looking far means looking back, and the young universe was very different from today's.

    What came before?

    It is one of the great unanswered questions. According to Einstein's relativity, time began with the Big Bang, so asking what came before might be like asking what lies north of the North Pole. But in the very first instants the laws we know break down, and we need a theory joining relativity and quantum physics, which we do not yet have. There are hypotheses of cyclic universes, a multiverse or an earlier bounce, but none has been confirmed.

    Did you know?

    A small part of the "snow" on old televisions with no signal was radiation from the cosmic background: an echo of the Big Bang in your living room.

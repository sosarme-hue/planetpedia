    Dark matter and dark energy

    95% of the universe is something we neither see nor understand: invisible matter that holds galaxies together and energy that speeds up the expansion.

    Everything we can see, touch or detect with instruments, stars, planets, gas, ourselves, is only around 5% of the universe. The rest is split between two great unknowns: dark matter (27%) and dark energy (68%).

    Dark matter: gravity without an owner

    In 1933 the Swiss astronomer Fritz Zwicky studied the Coma cluster of galaxies and saw that its galaxies moved so fast that the cluster should have flown apart. Far more mass was needed than could be seen. He called it dunkle Materie, dark matter. Hardly anyone paid attention.

    In the 1970s the American Vera Rubin measured the speed of stars in spiral galaxies. Those on the outskirts should move more slowly than those near the centre, as distant planets do around the Sun. But no: they spun just as fast. Galaxies were wrapped in an enormous halo of invisible mass. The great telescope that bears her name in Chile began observing in 2025.

    The evidence

    • Rotation curves of galaxies, as Vera Rubin discovered.
    • Gravitational lensing: clusters bend light far more than their visible mass could.
    • The Bullet Cluster: two clusters collided; the gas, which is nearly all their ordinary matter, was slowed in the middle, but most of the mass, measured by gravitational lensing, carried straight on. It is mass that neither collides nor shines.
    • The cosmic background and the structure of the universe: without dark matter, galaxies would not have had time to form.

    What is it?

    Nobody knows. It is not gas, dark dust or ordinary black holes: it does not emit, absorb or reflect light, and barely interacts with anything except through gravity. The favourite candidates are as-yet unknown particles, such as WIMPs or axions. Experiments buried under mountains, like the one at the Canfranc Underground Laboratory (Huesca, Spain), are trying to detect them. So far, without success.

    Dark energy: something pushing

    In 1998 it was discovered that the expansion of the universe is accelerating. Something acts like a kind of large-scale antigravity. It is called dark energy, and it may be a property of empty space itself, Einstein's "cosmological constant". The baffling part is that quantum physics predicts a value 10¹²⁰ times larger than the measured one, perhaps the worst prediction in the history of physics.

    In 2024 and 2025 the DESI project, which has mapped tens of millions of galaxies, found hints that dark energy may be weakening over time. If confirmed, it would be a revolution. Europe's Euclid space telescope, launched in 2023, is measuring billions of galaxies to settle the question.

    Did you know?

    Every second, billions of dark matter particles could be passing through your body without you noticing, just like neutrinos from the Sun.

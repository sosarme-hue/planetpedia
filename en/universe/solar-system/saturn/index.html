    Saturn

    The lord of the rings: a giant so light it would float in water, wrapped in ice and with more moons than any other planet.

    Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest, but what makes it unforgettable are its rings. It is the most distant planet visible to the naked eye, and ancient peoples linked it to the god of time because of its slow crawl across the sky: it takes almost 30 years to orbit the Sun.

    A giant that would float

    Saturn is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, like Jupiter. It has 95 times Earth's mass but takes up so much space that its average density is lower than water's: if there were a bathtub big enough, Saturn would float. It spins so quickly, in about ten and a half hours, that it is the most flattened planet, visibly squashed at the poles.

    The rings

    The rings stretch out to about 280,000 km from the planet, nearly three quarters of the distance between Earth and the Moon, yet they are generally only about 10 metres thick. They are made of trillions of chunks of almost pure water ice, from grains of dust to blocks the size of houses.

    Their age is debated. Data from the Cassini probe suggest they could be young, a few hundred million years old, perhaps the remains of a moon torn apart by the planet's gravity. They are also disappearing: ice is slowly falling onto Saturn as "ring rain".

    A hexagon at the pole

    At Saturn's north pole there is a jet stream shaped like an almost perfect hexagon, each side about 29,000 km long, bigger than two Earths. The Voyager probes discovered it in the 1980s and it is still there. It is thought to form from differences in speed between bands of wind.

    More moons than anyone

    Saturn is the planet with the most known moons: 274 confirmed by 2025. Two of them are among the most interesting in the solar system:

    • Titan, larger than Mercury, is the only moon with a thick atmosphere and the only place besides Earth with lakes and rivers on its surface, though of liquid methane and ethane.
    • Enceladus, just 500 km across, shoots jets of water into space from an ocean hidden under its ice.

    The Cassini–Huygens mission

    The Cassini probe, from NASA, ESA and the Italian space agency, orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017. It dropped Europe's Huygens lander onto Titan, the most distant landing in history, and discovered the geysers of Enceladus. When it ran out of fuel it plunged into Saturn's atmosphere so as not to contaminate its moons.

    Did you know?

    Every 13 to 15 years the rings are seen edge-on from Earth and almost vanish. Galileo, the first to see them in 1610, was baffled when they disappeared from view in 1612.
